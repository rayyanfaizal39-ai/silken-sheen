Three states, one particle theory Arrangement, movement, attraction, and spacing determine how a material behaves. Matter is made up of constantly moving small and discrete particles. When heat is supplied, particles move faster; when cooled, particles move slower. Experiment 5.6: conservation of mass 01 Ice melts Method Weigh a beaker of ice, allow it to melt completely, then weigh the same beaker again. Observation and conclusion Mass before melting equals mass after melting because no particles were lost. 02 Salt dissolves Method Weigh water and salt together, dissolve all the salt, then weigh the salt solution. Observation and conclusion The combined starting mass equals the mass of the final solution. 03 A solid expands Method Weigh a metal ball and ring, heat the ball until it no longer fits through the ring, then weigh them again. Observation and conclusion Heating increases particle spacing and volume, but mass and particle number remain unchanged. Use matter ideas in real life Apply evaporation, condensation, and particle theory to familiar situations. Everyday examples Freezing Sweet cream freezes to become ice-cream Sublimation Dry ice is used by ice-cream vendors to keep ice-cream cold without melting it Sublimation Moth balls shrinking over time — solid changing directly to gas Condensation Dew forms when water vapour in the air condenses into water droplets Active recall Q1 Why does a wet towel dry below 100°C? Evaporation occurs at any temperature at the surface; wind removes water vapour and speeds the process. Q2 How do particles move in each state? Solid particles vibrate in fixed positions, liquid particles slide past one another, and gas particles move randomly at high speed. Q3 Why is ethanol heated in a water bath? Ethanol is highly flammable, so direct heating over a flame is dangerous. Q4 How can seawater provide drinking water? Evaporate the seawater and cool the vapour on a plastic sheet so condensed water drips into a separate cup. Chapter rule Matter is conserved during physical changes: particles may move faster or slower, spread out or pack together, but they are not created or destroyed.
