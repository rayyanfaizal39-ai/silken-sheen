## HIGH

### H-01 · INCORRECT · atmospheric pressure attributed to weaker gravity at altitude

- **Location:** `interactive-bm.ts:57` (detail) · `interactive-dlp.ts:58`
- **Current (BM):** "Tekanan berkurang dengan altitud — **kurang graviti menarik udara ke
  bawah** bermakna udara kurang tumpat di sana."
- **Current (DLP):** "Pressure drops with altitude — **less gravity pulling the air down**
  means it's less dense up there."
- **Problem:** Gravity is essentially unchanged over the atmosphere's height (about 0.3%
  weaker at 10 km). Atmospheric pressure falls with altitude because there is **less air
  above** — pressure at any height is the weight of the overlying air column — and the air
  below is compressed by that column, which is why density falls too. Attributing it to
  weaker gravity teaches a misconception, in the chapter's primary notes surface, in **both
  languages**.
- **Source:** DSKP 8.2.7 requires "kesan altitud kepada tekanan atmosfera"; textbook p. 192
  defines atmospheric pressure as that exerted by the atmosphere on the Earth's surface and
  all bodies on it. Neither invokes varying gravity.
- **Severity:** HIGH.

### H-02 · CONFUSING/INCORRECT · live surfaces contradict each other on the flotation condition

- **Notes** (`interactive-bm.ts:100`): "Sesuatu objek terapung apabila daya keapungan yang
  menolaknya ke atas **sama dengan** beratnya" → **F = W**
- **Flashcards** (`flashcards-bm.ts:221`, `flashcards-dlp.ts:221`): "Terapung: objek kurang
  tumpat daripada cecair (**F > W**)"
- **Quizzes** (BM Q25, `quizzes-dlp.ts:367`): "…menyebabkan daya keapungan **F > berat W**"
- **Source:** the textbook uses both, under different conditions — printed p. 177: an object
  floats when "daya apungan **sama dengan** berat objek" (equilibrium); printed p. 179 Rajah
  8.13 labels **F > W** but only for an object "**cuba ditenggelamkan**" (while being pushed
  under), which is what drives it back up.
- **Problem:** AcadeMY's flashcard and quiz reproduce the p. 179 label **without its
  qualifying condition**, so as written they state that an object floating at rest has an
  unbalanced upward force. That contradicts the notes on the same chapter and is wrong as
  stated. The audit brief flags exactly this: *"Do not oversimplify floating to density alone
  if the live explanation contradicts the source's force relationship."*
- **Severity:** HIGH — a learner meets both claims and cannot reconcile them.
