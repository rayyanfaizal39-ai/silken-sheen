3.1

Transport System in Organisms

Why size demands a delivery system

Simple, unicellular organisms like Amoeba don't need a transport system — oxygen and nutrients diffuse directly through the cell membrane. But complex organisms like humans and plants are far too large for diffusion alone to reach every cell in time — so they evolved a specialised transport system.

🦠 Simple organisms

No specialised transport system
Substances diffuse directly through cell membrane

🌳 Complex organisms

Specialised transport system (heart + vessels, or xylem + phloem)
Diffusion alone is too slow across their large volume

Check yourself — 3.1

What happens to an organism if its transport system fails?Reveal hint
Toxic waste products that can't be eliminated from cells will build up and poison the organism — and cells will also be starved of the oxygen and nutrients they need.

Blood Circulatory System

Three vessels, one continuous loop

Human blood is pumped by the heart through arteries, capillaries and veins in a continuous circuit. Tap each vessel type.

The heart's four chambers

Tap each chamber to see its function.

🔴 Right atrium▾

Receives deoxygenated blood from the whole body (except lungs) via the vena cava, then forces it into the right ventricle.

🔴 Right ventricle▾

Pumps deoxygenated blood out through the pulmonary artery to the lungs.

🔵 Left atrium▾

Receives oxygenated blood from the lungs via the pulmonary vein, then forces it into the left ventricle.

🔵 Left ventricle▾

Has the thickest muscular wall — pumps oxygenated blood out through the aorta to the whole body except the lungs.

The 'lub-dub' sound of your own heartbeat

Systole ('Lub')
Diastole ('Dub')
Ventricles contract, closing the tricuspid and bicuspid valves — producing the 'lub' sound. The pressure reading of blood flowing OUT of the heart is the systolic pressure.
Ventricles relax, closing the semilunar valves at the aorta and pulmonary artery — producing the 'dub' sound. The pressure reading of blood flowing INTO the heart is the diastolic pressure.

What changes your pulse rate

🏃 Physical activity — more vigorous = higher rate⚧ Gender — women average 78–82 bpm, men 70–72 bpm🎂 Age — max pulse rate drops as you get older❤️ Body health — abnormally high or low rates can be dangerous

Check yourself — 3.2

Why is systolic pressure always higher than diastolic pressure?Reveal hint
Systolic pressure is measured during ventricular contraction, when blood is actively forced out under high pressure — diastolic is measured during relaxation, when pressure naturally drops.
3.3

Human Blood

Four components, one mixture

Blood is a suspension of red blood cells, white blood cells, platelets and blood plasma (about 90% water, carrying nutrients, hormones, enzymes and waste). Spin it in a centrifuge and it separates into a yellow liquid (plasma) floating over red blood cells.

🩸 Check blood type compatibility yourself

Pick a donor and recipient blood group to see if a transfusion would be safe.

Why compatibility matters

An antibody in the recipient's plasma attacks any matching antigen on the donor's red blood cells, causing blood to coagulate — which can be fatal. That's why O is the universal donor (no antigens to attack) and AB is the universal recipient (no antibodies to react).

Check yourself — 3.3

Why can't someone with type O blood receive any other blood type?Reveal hint
Type O plasma contains both Anti-A and Anti-B antibodies, which would attack the A or B antigens present in any other blood type.

Transport System in Plants

Transpiration — water's one-way trip upward

Transpiration is the loss of water vapour from a plant's surface — mostly through leaf stomata — via evaporation. This pulls water and dissolved minerals up from the roots through the xylem.

What speeds up or slows down transpiration

Tap each factor.

Two transport tissues, two jobs

🌊 Xylem

Transports water and dissolved mineral salts
Direction: roots → stem → leaves

🍯 Phloem

Transports sucrose from photosynthesis
Direction: leaves → all other plant parts

Check yourself — 3.4

Why is red dye (eosin) used to trace water movement in a plant experiment?Reveal hint
The dye travels along with the water through the xylem, making the normally invisible water pathway visible under a microscope after the stem is sliced.
3.5

Blood Circulatory System in Animals and Transport System in Plants

Same purpose, different design

❤️ Animals

Tubular system with a pump (heart) and valves
3 vessel types: artery, capillary, vein — all connected in one loop

🌿 Plants

System of vessels with no pump or valve
2 separate, unconnected vessels: xylem and phloem

Both are transport systems that carry water, nutrients and dissolved substances — and both exist because complex organisms are too large for diffusion alone.

Check yourself — 3.5

Give one similarity and one difference between animal blood circulation and plant transport.Reveal hint
Similarity: both transport water and dissolved substances in complex organisms. Difference: animals use one connected pumped loop; plants use two separate unconnected vessels with no pump.
