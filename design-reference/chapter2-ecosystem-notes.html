Energy Flow in an Ecosystem

Where does it all start?

Every ecosystem runs on sunlight. Green plants trap that light energy and convert it into chemical energy through photosynthesis — then pass it along, organism to organism, through a food chain.

From chain to web

Real ecosystems rarely run just one chain — a food web is several food chains interconnected. And at every link, some energy always leaks away as heat or undigested waste, so less and less energy reaches each higher level.

Check yourself — 2.1

Nutrient Cycle in an Ecosystem

Nothing is wasted in nature

Nutrients move in a loop — taken from the environment, used by living things, then returned to be used again. You met the water, carbon and oxygen cycles in Form 1; here we look at what role living things actually play in keeping them turning.

When human activity interferes

Logging, burning fossil fuels and overusing water resources all throw these cycles off balance. Tap each to see a way to fix it.

🪓 Unrestricted logging▾

Fix: Create planned agricultural systems and replant trees to restore what's cut.

🔥 Burning fossil fuels▾

Fix: Shift toward public transport to cut the carbon released into the cycle.

💧 Overconsuming water▾

Fix: Store rainwater for daily use and tighten laws around water resource use.

Role of Humans in Maintaining a Balanced Nature

What our activities cost nature

Tap an activity to see its real effect on the environment.

🪓 Forest logging▾

Extinction of flora and fauna species, soil erosion, worsened greenhouse effect.

🏭 Industrialisation▾

Air, water and soil pollution, acid rain, greenhouse effect.

🌾 Agriculture▾

Water pollution from overused pesticides and fertilisers; soil loses minerals under non-sustainable practices.

🗑️ Waste disposal▾

Water and ground pollution, foul odour from decaying waste, flash floods.
