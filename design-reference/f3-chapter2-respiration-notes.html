2.1

Human Respiratory System

The path air takes

Breathing is the process of inhaling and exhaling air by the lungs. Air travels: nostrils → nasal cavity → pharynx → larynx → trachea → bronchus → bronchiole → alveolus — where the actual gas exchange happens.

I can remember!The human respiratory system functions to supply oxygen and remove carbon dioxide from the body's cells.

🫁 Inhale or exhale?

Intercostal muscles contract, pulling the rib cage up and out. The diaphragm contracts and flattens downward. Together, this enlarges the thoracic cavity, lowering air pressure inside — so higher outside air pressure forces air into the lungs.
Intercostal muscles relax, and the rib cage moves down and in. The diaphragm relaxes and curves upward. This shrinks the thoracic cavity, raising air pressure inside — pushing air back out of the lungs.

Movement and Exchange of Gases in the Human Body

From alveolus to body cell, in six steps

Oxygen diffuses from the alveolus (higher concentration) into the blood, where haemoglobin forms an unstable compound called oxyhaemoglobin. At the body cells, oxyhaemoglobin decomposes to release oxygen for cellular respiration, and carbon dioxide diffuses back the other way.

Health of Human Respiratory System

What's in the air that hurts us

Cigarette tar, carbon monoxide, sulphur dioxide, nitrogen dioxide, haze, dust and pollen can all damage the respiratory system. Tap each to see how.

🚬 Cigarette tar▾

Sticks to and kills cells along the air passage, increases mucus production, and is a leading cause of lung cancer.

☠️ Carbon monoxide▾

Combines with haemoglobin to form stable carboxyhaemoglobin — this blocks oxygen transport, starving body cells of the energy they need.

🏭 Sulphur & nitrogen dioxide▾

Released from burning coal and vehicle fuel — irritates the air passage, causing coughing, breathing difficulty, bronchitis and asthma.

😷 Haze, dust & pollen▾

Fine solid particles suspended in the air that irritate the respiratory system and trigger asthma.

2.5

Gaseous Exchange in Plants

Plants breathe too — through their stomata

Gas exchange in plants happens mainly through stomata in the leaves, controlled by a pair of guard cells. Whether the stoma is open depends on osmosis.

☀️ Daytime
🌙 Night / Hot Day
Guard cells photosynthesise, raising their glucose concentration. Water diffuses in via osmosis, making guard cells turgid and curved — this opens the stoma.
Water diffuses out of guard cells via osmosis, making them flaccid and straight — this closes the stoma, reducing water loss.

Why unpolluted air matters to plants

Haze and dust settling on stomata block gaseous exchange and reduce photosynthesis. Acidic gases like sulphur dioxide dissolve in rain to form acid rain — killing plant cells and making soil less fertile.

Check yourself — 2.5

Why do stomata close on very hot days, even during daylight?Reveal hint
To limit water loss through transpiration — excessive heat can cause the plant to lose water faster than its roots can replace it.
