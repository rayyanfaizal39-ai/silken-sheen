⚽ Science Blog — Force Is All Around Us

Walking, chewing, kicking a ball, pulling a rope in tug-of-war — every one of these is force in action. You can't see it, but you can always feel its effect.

8.1

Force

What force actually is

Force is simply a push or a pull on an object. It shows up in almost everything you do — opening a can, pressing a switch, pushing open a door. Force comes in several forms. Tap each to see it in action.

Force has three properties

Force is a vector — meaning it always has magnitude (how strong), direction (which way), and a point of application (exactly where it acts). A 10 N push and a 10 N pull are the same magnitude but completely different forces.

How we measure itForce is measured in newtons (N) using a spring balance, which works on the principle of spring extension — the more the spring stretches, the greater the force. On Earth, 100 g of mass weighs 1 N, so 1 kg weighs 10 N.

Every action has an equal, opposite reaction

Whenever a force acts on something, an equal-magnitude force pushes back the other way. Tap each situation to see the pair.

📖 A book resting on a table▾

The book's weight (action) pulls down; the table's normal force (reaction) pushes up with equal magnitude — that balance is why the book stays still.

🪵 A block floating on water▾

Weight (action) pulls down; buoyant force (reaction) pushes up. Equal magnitude means it floats instead of sinking or launching upward.

🚃 Two trolleys pushed apart by a spring▾

The spring pushes each trolley away with equal force in opposite directions — so both trolleys travel the same distance, just in opposite directions.

Effects of Force

Five things force can do to an object

You can't see force directly, but you can always spot its effects. Tap each.

🌊 Why do some things float and others sink?

An object floats when the buoyant force pushing it up equals its weight. Whether that happens depends on density — compare the object's density to water's density (1.0 g/cm³). Try it yourself:

🧮 Moment of force calculator

The turning effect of a force is its moment: Moment = Force × perpendicular distance from the pivot. Try it — this is exactly how a spanner or a see-saw works.

— N m

🧮 Pressure calculator

The same force feels completely different depending on the area it's spread over: Pressure = Force ÷ Area. That's why a thumbtack pierces a plank but a coin under the same force doesn't — and why an elephant's huge feet keep it from sinking.

— Pa

Gas pressure: two factors

Gas pressure comes from air molecules constantly colliding with a container's walls. Tap each factor.

📦 Volume▾

Compress a container and molecules collide with the walls more often — pressure rises.

🌡️ Temperature▾

Heat the gas and molecules move faster, hitting the walls harder and more often — pressure rises.

Atmospheric pressure in daily life

Atmospheric pressure is just the air pressing down on everything on Earth's surface. It's why these everyday tools work:

🪠 Plunger — seals out air, atmospheric pressure holds it down🥤 Straw — sucking lowers pressure inside, outside pressure pushes drink up🧹 Vacuum cleaner — fan lowers internal pressure, air rushes in carrying dust
Higher up, thinner airAtmospheric pressure drops as altitude increases — fewer air molecules are pulled down by gravity up there. That's part of why breathing feels harder near a mountain peak.

Pressure underwater

Liquid pressure increases with depth — which is exactly why dam walls are built thicker at the base, why divers wear pressure-resistant suits, and why a submarine hull must be strong enough to resist being crushed at depth.

Check yourself — 8.2

A 5 N cuboid has three different faces. Which face gives the highest pressure on the ground?Reveal hint
The smallest-area face — smaller area with the same force always means higher pressure.
Why does a helium balloon rise and expand as it climbs higher?Reveal hint
It rises because helium is less dense than air. It expands because atmospheric pressure drops with altitude, letting the gas inside push outward more.
