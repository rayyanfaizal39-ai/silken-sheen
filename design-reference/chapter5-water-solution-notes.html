🌊 Science Blog — The Dead Sea

At 430.5 metres below sea level, the Dead Sea is the lowest point on Earth. Because it's landlocked, incoming river water evaporates fast and leaves salt behind — making the water so dense that people float on the surface without even trying.

5.1

Physical Characteristics of Water

What makes pure water "pure"

Pure water is colourless, odourless and tasteless, and stays liquid at room temperature. It boils at exactly 100°C, freezes at 0°C, and has a density of 1 g/cm³ — these fixed values are actually how we test whether water has anything dissolved in it, since impurities shift them.

Why insects can "walk" on waterWater molecules pull on each other at the surface — called surface tension — strongly enough that light insects like the daddy longlegs can rest on top without sinking. That same pulling force, working between water and a plant's xylem walls, is what drags water up from roots to leaves — a phenomenon called capillary action.

What water is actually made of

Water is a compound — two hydrogen atoms bonded to one oxygen atom, H₂O. Split it apart using electrolysis and you get the proof: oxygen gas collects at the anode, hydrogen gas at the cathode, in a 1:2 ratio of oxygen to hydrogen by volume.

Changing state

Water shifts between solid, liquid and gas by absorbing or releasing heat. Tap each change to see which direction the heat moves.

Why does salty soup take longer to boil?

Dissolved impurities change water's melting and boiling points — salt actually lowers the melting point of ice but raises the boiling point of water. That's why a pot of plain water boils faster than one with salt or soup stock in it.

What speeds up evaporation

Evaporation happens right at the water's surface, at any temperature — it's just the fastest-moving molecules escaping into the air. Four factors change how quickly that happens. Tap each to see how.

5.2

Solution and Rate of Solubility

Solute, solvent, solution

When sugar dissolves in water, the sugar is the solute (the substance dissolving), water is the solvent (the liquid doing the dissolving), and sugar water is the solution they form together.

Add more and more solute, and the solution moves through three stages:

💧 Dilute

Little solute
Can dissolve much more

🧪 Concentrated

More solute
Can still dissolve a bit more

⚗️ Saturated

Excess solute
No more dissolves — forms a precipitate

Solution, suspension, or colloid?

Not everything that mixes with water actually dissolves. Tap each type to see what tells them apart.

Solution
Suspension
Colloid
Solute particles are tiny enough to disperse evenly — the mixture looks clear/transparent, light passes through, and nothing is left behind when filtered. E.g. copper sulphate dissolved in water.
Solute particles are too large to dissolve — the mixture looks cloudy, blocks light, settles over time, and leaves a residue when filtered. E.g. chalk powder in water, or muddy river water.
Particles are in between — evenly dispersed but neither fully clear nor settling out. E.g. milk and mayonnaise (emulsions), shaving foam (foam).

Why water dissolves almost anything

Water is called the universal solvent because it dissolves such a huge range of solids, liquids and gases — used everywhere from fertiliser uptake in plant roots to soft drink production. For substances water can't dissolve, we turn to organic (carbon-based) solvents instead.

1. Why is hot water better for dissolving coffee than cold water?Reveal hint
Higher temperature gives particles more energy to move and mix faster — increasing the rate of solubility.
2. Milk looks uniform but isn't a true solution. What is it?Reveal hint
A colloid — an emulsion, specifically. It doesn't separate like a suspension, but it's not a clear solution either.
5.3

Water Purification and Water Supply

Why raw water isn't safe to drink

Even though water covers two-thirds of Earth's surface, most of it carries impurities, microorganisms and dissolved substances that make it unsafe straight from the source. Four core methods clean it up. Tap each to see what it removes.

🔥 Boiling▾

Kills microorganisms through heat.

🧪 Chlorination▾

Chlorine added to kill microorganisms in the water supply.

🧻 Filtration▾

Separates suspended particles like leaves and sediment from the water.

♨️ Distillation▾

Separates suspended particles AND dissolved substances, while also killing microorganisms — the most thorough method.
