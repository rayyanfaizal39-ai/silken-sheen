🚀 Science Blog — Feeding an Astronaut

When Malaysia's first astronaut, Sheikh Muszaphar, flew to the ISS in 2007, MARDI had to reformulate his meals — salt behaves differently in space, so recipes were tested repeatedly until the food stayed both nutritious and appetising, then frozen in air-tight containers.

Classes of Food

Seven building blocks

Every meal is really just a mix of seven food classes, each doing a different job in your body. Tap one to see what it's for.

Vitamins & minerals — small amounts, big consequences

Vitamins and minerals don't supply energy, but skipping them causes very specific problems. Tap to expand.

👁️ Vitamin A — night vision, skin health▾

Deficiency causes night blindness and skin diseases. Found in milk, egg yolk, fish oil.

🧠 Vitamin B — nervous system, red blood cells▾

Deficiency causes beri-beri and anaemia. Found in yeast, liver, eggs.

🦷 Vitamin C — immunity, gum health▾

Deficiency causes scurvy (bleeding gums). Found in fruits and vegetables.

🦴 Vitamin D — calcium absorption, bones▾

Deficiency causes rickets. From butter, eggs, fish oil, and sun exposure.

🦴 Calcium — bones, blood-clotting▾

Deficiency causes rickets and osteoporosis. Found in milk, anchovies, prawns, green vegetables.

🩸 Iron — haemoglobin formation▾

Deficiency causes anaemia. Found in liver and meat.

3.4

Absorption, Transport and Defecation

Villi — nature's surface-area trick

The small intestine wall is covered in millions of tiny finger-like villi, each just one cell thick. That thinness plus the huge surface area they create means digested nutrients pass into the bloodstream fast.

Brain TeaserWhy does the small intestine have more blood vessels than the stomach? More absorption happening there means more blood needed to carry nutrients away.

Where it all ends up

Glucose becomes energy. Amino acids build new cells. Fatty acids and glycerol recombine into fat for insulation and organ protection. This whole distribution process is called assimilation.

💩 What happens to the leftovers?▾

Undigested fibre, waste and dead cells move into the large intestine, where water is reabsorbed — leaving solid faeces, stored in the rectum, then eliminated through the anus (defecation).

Check yourself — 3.4

What is reabsorbed by the large intestine?Reveal hint
