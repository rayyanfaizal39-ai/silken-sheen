Four ways infectious disease travels

Tap a route to see how it spreads and how it's stopped.

🌬️ Airborne — droplets & dust▾

Examples: tuberculosis, flu, SARS, H1N1, chickenpox.
Prevent: cover coughs/sneezes, avoid crowded spaces, let in sunlight (UV kills some microbes).

💧 Waterborne — contaminated supply▾

Examples: cholera, typhoid, amoebic dysentery.
Prevent: chlorinate water, build proper sanitation, boil drinking water, wash hands after the toilet.

🤝 Contact — touch or fluids▾

Examples: ringworm, tinea (skin contact); syphilis, gonorrhoea, HIV (bodily fluids).

🦟 Vector — carried by animals▾

Examples: dengue and Zika (Aedes mosquito), malaria (Anopheles mosquito), leptospirosis (rat), typhoid (cockroach/fly).

Three stages of prevention

1️⃣ Primary — improve & strengthen▾

Better hygiene and sanitation, vaccination and immunisation, regular check-ups, healthy lifestyle.

2️⃣ Secondary — detect & treat▾

Active/passive case detection, early treatment, isolating patients from others.

3️⃣ Tertiary — control & protect▾

Destroy vector breeding grounds, fogging, enforce hygiene laws, use mosquito nets/coils, wear protective clothing.

Immunity: two types, two origins

Passive
Active
The body receives ready-made antibodies from outside — naturally through breast milk/placenta, or artificially through an antiserum injection. Fast, but temporary and short-lived.
The body produces its own antibodies after antigen exposure — naturally by recovering from infection, or artificially through vaccination. Slower to build, but lasts much longer.

Check yourself — 4.2

1. Amin had chickenpox at age 7 and never gets it again. What type of immunity is this?Reveal hint
Active natural immunity — his own body produced antibodies after recovering from the real infection.
2. A baby gets antibodies from breast milk. What type of immunity is this?Reveal hint
Passive natural immunity — the antibodies came from an outside source (the mother), not the baby's own immune response.
