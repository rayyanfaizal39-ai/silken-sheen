10.1

Characteristics of Sound Waves

Sound is vibration, and it needs a ride

Every sound starts as a vibration — your vocal cords, a guitar string, a speaker cone. But sound can't travel through nothing: it needs a medium (solid, liquid, or gas) to propagate, which is exactly why there's no sound in the vacuum of space.

🪨 Solids

Fastest — particles packed tightly together

💧 Liquids

Slower — particles more loosely arranged

💨 Gases

Slowest — particles far apart

Reflection and absorption

Sound bounces off some surfaces and gets soaked up by others — just like light. Tap each.

Hard & smooth
Soft & rough
Marble tiles and bare walls are good sound reflectors — sound bounces right back, which is what creates an echo.
Carpet and softboard are good sound absorbers — that's why cinema hall walls are lined with soft boards, to cut down on echo.

10.2

Loudness and Pitch of Sound

Two knobs control every sound

Loudness depends on amplitude — how big the vibration is. Pitch depends on frequency — how fast it vibrates, measured in hertz (Hz). A cow's low moo is low frequency; a rat's squeak is high frequency.

The Doppler effect

Ever notice an ambulance siren sound higher-pitched as it approaches, then drop lower as it passes? That's the Doppler effect — sound waves bunch up in front of a moving source (higher frequency) and stretch out behind it (lower frequency).

Check yourself — 10.2

A musician plays a very soft note. What characteristic changed?Reveal hint
Amplitude — a softer note means a smaller vibration, which is lower loudness, not a change in pitch.
10.3

Phenomenon and Application of Reflection

Echo: sound bouncing back

An echo happens when a sound wave reflects off a hard surface and reaches your ears again, slightly delayed — common in empty halls, caves, tunnels and gorges.

Beyond human hearing

Ultrasound is sound above 20,000 Hz — too high for human ears, but not for bats, which use it to navigate in the dark. Reflected sound waves power real technology too:

🚢 Sonar — detects underwater objects for ships 🏥 Medical ultrasound — images inside the body 🎣 Fisheries — locates schools of fish 🦇 Bat echolocation — natural navigation in the dark

Check yourself — 10.3

Why do cinema halls line their walls with soft boards instead of leaving them bare?Reveal hint
Soft boards absorb sound instead of reflecting it, cutting down unwanted echo so dialogue and music stay clear.
