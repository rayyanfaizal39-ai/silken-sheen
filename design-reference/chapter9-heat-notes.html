9.1

Relationship between Temperature and Heat

Heat and temperature aren't the same thing

Heat is a form of energy that flows from a hotter region to a colder one — measured in joules. Temperature is just the degree of hotness or coldness — measured in °C or kelvin. Two beakers of water at the same temperature can hold very different amounts of heat, depending on how much water there is.

🔥 Heat

Heat Flow and Thermal Equilibrium

Three ways heat travels

Heat always moves from hot to cold, through one of three routes. Tap each.

🌬️ Why the coast gets a breeze — day vs night

Land and sea heat up and cool down at different rates — that difference drives a real convection current you can feel. Tap to switch between day and night.

☀️ Daytime
🌙 Nighttime
Sea breeze: the Sun heats land faster than sea. Warm air over land rises, and cooler, denser air from the sea rushes in to replace it — creating a breeze blowing from sea to land.
Land breeze: land cools faster than sea at night. Warmer air over the sea rises, and cooler air from land moves out to replace it — creating a breeze blowing from land to sea.

When heat flow stops

Two objects in contact keep exchanging heat until there's no more net transfer between them — at that point they're in thermal equilibrium and share the same temperature.

Why is the heating coil in an electric kettle placed at the bottom?Reveal hint

9.3

Principle of Expansion and Contraction of Matter

Heat makes particles move — and spread out

In a solid, heating makes particles vibrate faster and push further apart — the object expands. Cooling does the reverse — particles move closer and it contracts. Liquids and gases work the same way, just with particles that are already free to move.

Built to expand — on purpose

Engineers design around this principle constantly. Tap each example.

🚂 Railway track gaps▾

Small gaps between rail sections let tracks expand in heat without buckling.

🌉 Steel bridge rollers▾

One end sits on rollers with a gap, letting the whole bridge expand safely in hot weather.

🔔 Bimetallic strip fire alarm▾

Two different metals expand at different rates when heated — the strip bends toward a contact point, completing a circuit that triggers the alarm.

🌡️ Mercury thermometers▾

Mercury expands and contracts predictably with temperature, which is exactly what makes it useful for measuring heat.

Check yourself — 9.3

A bottle lid is stuck tight. How does the expansion principle help open it?Reveal hint
Submerging the lid in hot water makes the metal expand slightly, loosening its grip so it twists off more easily.

Surface Type and Heat Absorption/Emission

Dark and dull beats light and shiny

How well a surface absorbs or radiates heat depends on its colour and texture. Tap each type.

Dark, dull surface
Light, shiny surface
Absorbs and radiates heat well. That's why solar water heaters use dark panels, and why a black car feels hotter inside on a sunny day.
Absorbs and radiates heat poorly — it reflects more instead. That's exactly why fuel tank trucks are painted white or silver, to keep fuel from evaporating in the heat.
