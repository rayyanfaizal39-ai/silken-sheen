6.1

Properties of Acids and Alkalis

Where the words come from

"Acid" comes from the Latin acidus, meaning sour. "Alkali" comes from the Arabic al-qali, meaning ashes of plants. Both properties only show up in the presence of water — a dry solid acid or alkali won't turn litmus paper at all.

How we detect acid or alkali without tasting

An indicator changes colour depending on whether it touches something acidic, neutral, or alkaline. Tap each indicator.

🌸 Phenolphthalein▾

Colourless in acid, colourless when neutral, turns pink in alkali.

🌈 Universal indicator▾

Red in acid, green when neutral, blue in alkali — and every shade in between shows the actual pH value.

🟠 Methyl orange▾

Red in acid, yellow when neutral, yellow in alkali.

📄 Litmus paper▾

Blue litmus turns red in acid; red litmus turns blue in alkali — simple but doesn't show strength.

6.2

Neutralisation

When acid meets alkali

Mix an acid with an alkali and they cancel each other out — the acid loses its acidity, the alkali loses its alkalinity, and the reaction produces salt and water. This is neutralisation, and the precise lab technique used to carry it out is called titration.

Different pairs make different salts:

HCl + NaOH → Sodium chlorideH₂SO₄ + KOH → Potassium sulphateHNO₃ + NaOH → Sodium nitrate

Neutralisation is everywhere

Tap each everyday example to see the acid-alkali pair at work.

🦷 Toothpaste▾

Mouth bacteria produce acid that erodes teeth — alkaline toothpaste neutralises it.

💇 Shampoo + conditioner▾

Alkaline shampoo leaves hair slightly alkaline; a mildly acidic conditioner neutralises the residue, keeping hair smooth.

🧴 Face care▾

Alkaline cleansers can dry out skin — an acidic toner neutralises it afterward.

🌾 Soil treatment▾

Acidic soil is treated with alkaline slaked lime so crops can grow well.

🏭 Industrial waste▾

Acidic factory waste is treated with alkalis before being released into rivers.

Check yourself — 6.2

Amran got stung by a jellyfish. His pain worsened when soap (alkaline) was applied. Why?Reveal hint
If the jellyfish sting is acidic, adding an alkali doesn't automatically help — the reaction itself, or an incomplete/incorrect neutralisation, can actually irritate the wound further. Proper treatment depends on knowing the sting's actual chemistry.
