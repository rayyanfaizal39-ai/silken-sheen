## 2. The two cards against that source

Both answer the same question: *"Mengapakah permukaan Bulan yang sama sentiasa menghadap Bumi?"*

| | Answer | Equality of periods | Value |
|---|---|---|---|
| **source** | — | **sama** | **lebih kurang 27 hari** |
| **f34** | "Kerana tempoh Bulan berputar pada paksinya dan mengorbit Bumi adalah **hampir sama**, iaitu **27 hari**." | hampir sama | 27 hari |
| **f62** | "Kerana tempoh putaran Bulan pada paksinya dan tempoh peredarannya mengelilingi Bumi adalah **sama**, iaitu **lebih kurang 27 hari**." | sama | lebih kurang 27 hari |

`f62` is a near-verbatim reproduction of the textbook sentence.

`f34` carries the same mechanism and the same number, but moves the hedge: it approximates the
*equality* ("hampir sama") and states the *value* flat ("27 hari"), where the source does the
reverse. Both readings are true — the Moon is in synchronous rotation, so the periods are equal,
and the period is 27.3 days, which "27 hari" rounds and "lebih kurang 27 hari" hedges.

## 3. Verification of the four required points

**Both answers scientifically correct — YES.**
Synchronous (tidally locked) rotation is exactly the mechanism both cards state: rotation period
= orbital period ⇒ the same hemisphere faces Earth. `f34`'s "hampir sama" is a slightly softer
claim than the physics warrants, not a false one; `f34`'s bare "27 hari" is a rounding of 27.3,
not a wrong figure.

**Neither contradicts the textbook — CONFIRMED.**
`f62` matches p. 261 almost word for word. `f34` states the same relationship and the same
duration; it differs from the source only in which of the two quantities carries the
approximation. Neither asserts anything the page denies.

**Neither has a wrong answer/key — CONFIRMED.**
These are two-sided flashcards (`front` / `back`), not objective items — there is no option list
and therefore no key that can point at the wrong option. Each `back` answers its own `front`
correctly. Verified in the shipped data, not from the file text alone.

**No DLP concept missing because of the duplication — CONFIRMED, and the earlier parity note was wrong.**
The previous report stated the DLP deck had no equivalent duplicate, implying a BM/DLP
asymmetry. That was an artifact of exact-string matching: the two DLP fronts differ by one word
("the same **side**" vs "the same **surface**"), so they were not detected as identical.

The DLP deck carries the same pair:

| | DLP |
|---|---|
| `f34` | "Why does the same side of the Moon always face the Earth?" → "Because the time for the Moon to rotate on its axis and orbit Earth is **about the same**, 27 days." |
| `f62` | "Why does the same surface of the Moon always face Earth?" → "Because the time the Moon takes to rotate on its axis and the time it takes to revolve around Earth are **the same**, about 27 days." |

The BM and DLP pairs mirror each other precisely, including which card hedges which quantity.
Across the whole Moon topic: **7 cards in BM, 7 in DLP, identical ids, no card in one deck only.**
All four sub-concepts (rotation on axis · revolution around Earth · ~27 days · same face toward
Earth) are present on the DLP interactive notes, mind map and deck. **Nothing is displaced.**
