## Experiment 6.2 (pp. 172–174)

1. Surface: copper/carbon rods rubbed with sandpaper; observe appearance.
2. Ductility: bend copper wire into a circle; repeat with pencil graphite/lead.
3. Malleability: hammer iron, copper and sulphur pieces on a wooden block.
4. Electrical conductivity: dry cell, switch, ammeter and crocodile clips; compare iron, carbon and sulphur rods. No bulb substituted for the ammeter.
5. Heat conductivity: carbon, iron and copper rods clamped on a retort stand; candle heats one end; wax holds a thumbtack at the other; observe time to fall. No numerical timings supplied.
6. Melting: tin and sulphur powders in a crucible over a pipeclay triangle/tripod and Bunsen burner; thermometer range 0–360°C is apparatus specification, not an invented result. Retain the fume-chamber warning.

The printed results table is blank. Show qualitative relationships from Figure 6.8 as learning conclusions, not recorded experimental measurements. Do not fabricate readings, timings or melting temperatures.
